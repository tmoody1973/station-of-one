# 006 — Every aired story gets its own page, with a publisher photo or a machine illustration

**Decision** — Each aired story gets a web page with a short article written only from its own posts, its sources, Jev's score, a Listen button and an image: the publisher's preview photo when the linked article has one, otherwise an abstract Bauhaus illustration generated for the story and labeled as such.

**Why this came up** — Judges who click a headline should land on a real article with its sources one click away; that is the station's honesty pitch. But only 2 of the last 45 aired stories came with a photo (4%), so the pages, and the front page, looked empty. Getting this wrong either way has a cost: copying publishers' photos breaks decision 004, and generated images that look like news photos would mislead.

**Options**
1. **Publisher photos only.** Real photos, almost free (one Apify run an hour, $0.0009 in the spike). Cost: stories from Reddit, Bluesky and Hacker News threads have none, so many stories still have no image, and a hotlinked photo can break.
2. **Illustrations only.** Every story gets one, one consistent look, no rights questions. Cost: about $0.03–0.07 per image, and a machine picture where a real one existed.
3. **Photo, else illustration.** Most coverage. Cost: two looks side by side, and two new paid calls each hour.

**What we chose and why** — Option 3. Tarik made the call. Photos stay linked from the publisher's own address, credited (decision 004). Illustrations are abstract shapes in the station's colours, never people, text or logos, and every one carries the line "Illustration generated by machine for this story. Not a photograph."

**What we gave up** — A uniform look; about $2–4 a day at most in image generation; an Apify actor that needed full permission on the account; one more pipeline step that can fail (the station manager may skip it, and pages then fall back to the summary and the beat card).

**How we'll know if this was right** — Over a full day, at least 90% of aired stories show an image on their page; no illustration contains a person, a face or readable text; the story step never blocks a newscast.
